## Problem Statement

A California potter who has outgrown their community-studio membership (comfortable on the wheel, tired of cone 6 electric, curious about wood, soda, salt, raku, pit or gas reduction, or just needing a gas or electric kiln to rent) has no single place to find out what is happening and how to get in. Today the information is scattered across stale maps (Simon Levin's World Wood Kiln Map: 209 pins, no entry after 2019), small hobby directories (Flash & Ash, roughly 100 wood kilns), a free rental map with no type filter or dates (Kiln Share: about 1,288 kilns, about 15k visits a month), and word of mouth in Facebook groups, Instagram and newsletters. Nobody maps soda or salt kilns at all. Background research: `reports/Ceramic kiln map market research.md` in the repo.

Existing maps go stale because they list *kilns* and depend on owners to keep them current. Owners of oversubscribed private kilns have no reason to do that.

**Reframe adopted in this session:** the thing being listed is the *joinable opportunity* (a workshop, community firing, crew call or stoke shift, residency slot, or rental or firing service), not the kiln. Organizers already publicize these because they need participants, so AI collection works with their incentives. Kilns become the background map.

## Users (revised after approval)

Not beginners. Membership studios are growing, so more and more members will want to move past the studio electric kiln toward advanced firing.

1. **The outgrowing studio member (primary).** Has 1–5 years at a community studio, fires cone 6 electric, and wants atmospheric and alternative firing but doesn't know who fires what, when, or how outsiders get in. This person wants a *step up*, not an introduction.
2. **Membership studios (secondary).** Studios that partner with a nearby wood or alternative-fire kiln can offer a group firing as a membership perk. That keeps advanced members from leaving and gives the studio something to market. The tool shows them which kilns host outside groups and how to arrange it.
3. **Kiln owners (third).** They get volunteers and crew for stoke shifts, buyers for firing services and kiln rentals, and full workshops, plus a way to reach studios that could bring a whole group.

## Product Vision (context, not Phase 1 scope)

**How might we build tools that shrink the gap between a ceramics degree and the everyday potter?** A degree gives two things a studio membership doesn't:
- **Knowledge:** glaze chemistry, technique and firing theory. The existing **Ask About Glazes** assistant (`site/ask.html`, `functions/api/chat.ts`, Vectorize index `ceramics-prose`) is the start of this.
- **Access:** university wood, soda and salt kilns and firing crews. This firings list is the start of that.

The two should link up. When someone asks the glaze assistant "how do I prep pots for a soda firing?", it can point to upcoming California soda firings. Each firing listing can link to a glaze or technique explainer for its firing type. Both tools share the same AI extraction, the Vectorize knowledge base and the Flaming Clay brand. Later ideas (not committed): technique guides by firing type, shared firing logs and results, critique and mentorship from kiln owners.

## What Makes This Cool

- A studio member ready for more opens one page and sees "firings you can join in the next 90 days" across California: what it is, when, the cost, how to sign up, and whether it suits someone on their first atmospheric firing.
- **Crew calls.** Wood kiln owners need people to stoke more than they need customers. A "Help needed: stoke shifts and crew" section gives private kiln owners a reason to be listed, and gives outgrowing members time-for-access trades ("8-hour stoke shift, trade: half a shelf + meals, first wood firing OK").
- **A progression path** shown on listings, the step up from the studio: studio electric → raku or pit (one day) → soda or salt → stoking a wood kiln → firing your own work.
- **For studios:** kilns marked "hosts outside groups," so a membership studio can arrange a member firing.
- It lives on flamingclay.com, with Robby's own firings pinned as example listings. The person who keeps California's firing list becomes the connector in the scene.

## Constraints

- **Not a business.** It succeeds by being useful and by promoting Flaming Clay. No listing fees, ads or booking commissions.
- **California only** for the test.
- **Scope covers every firing type a potter can join or rent:** wood (anagama, noborigama, train and others), soda, salt, raku, pit/barrel/saggar, gas reduction, and electric or gas kiln rental and firing services.
- **Robby's time:** about 10–15 minutes a week of review, moving to auto-publish after a month or two once accuracy is proven.
- **Stack:** the existing Cloudflare Pages site (`site/`, `functions/api/*.ts`). Push to `main` deploys. D1 (`CERAMICS_DB`), KV (`GALLERY_CACHE`, `RATE_LIMIT`) and Workers AI bindings already exist in `wrangler.jsonc`. Never run `wrangler pages deploy` by hand.
- **No Instagram or Facebook scraping.** Instagram can come later only through Meta's Business Discovery API.
- **No CAPTCHA bypassing,** including on newsletter signup forms.

## Premises (agreed)

1. The listing is a joinable opportunity, not a kiln. Kilns are the background map.
2. *(Revised 2026-10-04 after the 12-month lookback; see "Lookback findings".)* California's wood/soda access is **small and full**: about 10 places, and every wood or soda firing found was sold out or waitlisted. The tool's value is therefore **not** "find an open slot." It is (a) knowing *when sign-up opens* and how to get on waitlists, (b) routes in that don't fill up the same way (firing memberships, public-rate college classes, enrollment, residencies), and (c) helping membership studios and kilns create *new* supply through partner firings.
3. *(Revised after the second opinion.)* Sources are websites and event pages, plus a newsletter inbox that subscribes itself, plus a short submit form. No social-media scraping. **The inbox comes in the Phase 2 build. The prototype is websites only, and its count is read with that in mind.**
4. Robby's review is the safety net. Auto-publish turns on only after about 4 weeks or about 50 reviewed items with 95%+ approved unchanged, and only for sources he has already approved.
5. It lives on flamingclay.com and promotes him through his own firings and, later, a "new firings near you" email.
6. Every listing links to the organizer's own page. Owners can opt out. Private home kilns show an approximate area (shifted about 5 km), never an address.

Nearly all are in Northern California.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Count first + crew-call section (CHOSEN)
- **What it is:** Hand-seed about 60 California sources. A local script uses Claude to pull out opportunities into JSON. Robby reviews by moving files in git. A `firings.html` page and map show approved items, with a crew-call section and the progression path.
- **Effort:** S–M (human: ~1 week, mostly hand-seeding sources / CC: ~1–2 sessions). **Risk:** Low.
- **Pros:** tests premise #2 within days; a real page on the site right away; nothing to maintain until it proves out.
- **Cons:** crawls are run by hand; no inbox yet, so newsletter-only crew calls are missed; reviewing in git is clunky.

### Approach B: Full pipeline on Cloudflare
- **What it is:**
  - A weekly scheduled crawl that spots page changes.
  - A newsletter inbox that subscribes itself, via an Email Worker that also clicks confirmation links.
  - Opportunities stored in D1, with a review page that shows the source quote beside each item.
  - A submit form protected by Turnstile, the "near you" email, and auto-publish once accuracy is proven.
- **Effort:** L (human: ~3–4 weeks / CC: ~4–6 sessions). **Risk:** Med.
- **Becomes Phase 2** if the prototype passes.

### Approach C: Crew-call board first
- **What it is:** A board seeded by Robby's outreach to potters he knows, with crawling filling in behind it.
- **Effort:** M. **Risk:** Med.
- **Not chosen as the lead,** because it depends on owners posting, the habit that made the existing maps go stale. Its crew-call section and progression path are folded into A.

## Recommended Approach

Approach A, with C's crew-call section and progression path.

**Prototype (Phase 1)**

1. **`kiln-map/sources.json`**: about 60 California sources, each with `url, org, kind (college|guild|city_arts|studio|private_kiln|residency), firing_types_guess[], public|private, approx_lat, approx_lng, region, newsletter_url?`. Seeded from:
   - CARB and district BACT searches for "ceramic kiln" / "wood-fired kiln"
   - California Community Colleges programs with TOP code 1002.30, and IPEDS programs with CIP 50.0711 in CA
   - ProPublica nonprofit search (ceramic / pottery / clay, CA), filtered by hand
   - city arts center class catalogs in the Bay Area, Sacramento, LA and San Diego
   - California entries from Kiln Share and Flash & Ash, plus Levin pins in CA
   - Robby's own list of kilns he knows

   Building-permit and business-registry hits are used for discovery only and never published.
   Each source also stores a fixed **`display_lat`/`display_lng`**, computed once with a random offset of about 5 km for private kilns, so repeated builds never reveal the true spot.
2. **`kiln-map/extract.mjs`**, run locally with the Anthropic API (`ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` in a local `.env`; add `.env` to `.gitignore` first, since it isn't there today; Haiku 4.5 for extraction). It:
   - fetches each URL plus linked events or workshop pages, one link deep. Same domain, plus an allow-list of catalog hosts (ActiveNet, CivicRec, Eventbrite).
   - marks sources that render only with JavaScript as `js_only` in COUNT.md instead of guessing.
   - calls Claude with a strict JSON schema: `title, firing_type (enum: wood|soda|salt|raku|pit_barrel_saggar|gas_reduction|electric|rental_service|other), kiln_style?, start_date, end_date?, date_precision (exact|month|season), signup_deadline?, cost_text, how_to_join, first_atmospheric_ok (bool), crew_needed (bool), hosts_groups (bool), registration_status (open|sold_out|waitlist|not_yet_open|unknown), registration_opens?, access_kind (dated|ongoing_membership|class_enrollment|residency|rental_service), source_id, source_url, source_quote (verbatim), confidence`. Region and location come from joining `source_id` to `sources.json`.
   - for `date_precision` of month or season, uses the first day of the period for the 90-day window and the last day for expiry.
   - names each file with a **stable id**: a hash of the normalized title, the host organizer (the org running the firing, not the page it was found on) and the start month. Before writing, it fuzzy-matches against existing items (same region, similar title, start dates within ±3 days), so the same firing found on a college, guild and organizer page becomes one item. When a matched item's date changes, the file is updated in place and goes back to `pending/` for review, instead of becoming a duplicate. The run skips any id already present in `pending/`, `approved/` or `rejected/`, which makes reruns safe.
   - stores the raw extraction under an `_original` field in each file.
   - keeps past items from the last 12 months, marked `past: true`, so COUNT.md can measure the yearly rate. `build.mjs` never publishes them.

   Use the IPv4-first Node flags from the project learnings (`--dns-result-order=ipv4first --network-family-autoselection-attempt-timeout=3000`) and `AbortSignal.timeout` on each fetch.
3. **Review:** move a file from `pending/` to `approved/` (or `rejected/`, with a one-word reason), editing fields if needed, then commit. `review-stats.mjs` compares each approved file against its `_original` field to count approved-unchanged / edited / rejected. That record is the gate for auto-publish later.
4. **Publish (only after the COUNT.md decision passes):** `kiln-map/build.mjs` writes `site/data/firings.json` from `approved/`, dropping anything past its `end_date` (or `start_date` if there's no end date). It joins each item to its source for region and display location. Robby runs it and commits the output, so `deploy.yml` needs no changes. **`site/firings.html`** (styled to DESIGN.md) shows:
   - filters for firing type, region and "next 90 days" (the same window as the success criteria), plus "first atmospheric firing OK" and "hosts studio groups" toggles
   - a "Help needed: crew calls" section first
   - a short progression-path strip
   - a Leaflet map with private kilns shifted about 5 km, using a tile provider whose usage policy fits (OSM attribution or a free-tier provider)
   - on each card: "found on [source], checked [date]" and an "Is this wrong?" mailto link

   Robby's own firing is pinned as the example listing. Link the page from the site nav.
5. **Count report:** after the first full run, `kiln-map/COUNT.md` gives:
   - per source: did it produce anything, the next firing found, and whether a sign-up date or status was found
   - totals for the past 12 months and next 90 days, by firing type
   - registration-status breakdown (open / sold out / waitlist / not yet open)
   - ongoing access routes found
   - extraction approval rate

**Phase 2 (only if the prototype passes)**

1. Move the opportunities into D1 (`CERAMICS_DB`, new `firing_sources` and `opportunities` tables). Serve `functions/api/firings.ts`, cached in KV with the same short-freshness pattern as `gallery.ts`.

## Success Criteria

- **Prototype pass (revised 2026-10-04): three gates, one per risk**
  1. **AI keeps up with the ~10 core wood/soda places:** for at least 8 of them, the run finds the next firing (or confirms none is posted) **and** its registration status or sign-up date, with at least 80% of items approved unchanged.
  2. **Outgrowing members want sign-up-opens alerts:** show the prototype list to studio members at 2–3 membership studios; 5+ people ask for alerts or a waitlist reminder.
  3. **A partner firing is plausible:** at least 1 membership studio is willing to talk with 1 listed kiln about a group firing.
- Gate 1 is a build test, run by the prototype. Gates 2 and 3 are conversations Robby has using the prototype page.
- **Dropped gate:** "10 joinable opportunities in 90 days." Raku and rentals clear it alone, so it proves nothing, and wood/soda slots are full anyway.
- **Two separate accuracy gates:** 80% approved unchanged is enough to pass the prototype. 95% over about 50 items is required before auto-publish.
- **Phase 2 health (needs the weekly scheduled crawl):** 15 minutes a week or less of Robby's time; no listing more than 14 days past its "checked" date; auto-publish turned on within about 8 weeks.
- **Usefulness signal:** at least one studio member joins a firing they found on the page; at least one owner submits a crew call without being asked; at least one membership studio asks about arranging a group firing.
- **Promotion signal:** firings.html becomes a top-3 entry page on flamingclay.com, or sends measurable traffic to the shop/gallery.

## Weekly cycle and future sources (added 2026-10-04)

**Weekly cycle (about 15 minutes of Robby's time):** Claude runs `npm run kiln:extract`, then shows the pending items as a short numbered plain-language list (place, what, date, status, quote). Robby replies with the numbers to reject or fix; Claude moves files, runs `npm run kiln:build`, commits and pushes (only on his "publish"). Every rejection carries a reason so the extraction rules can be tightened.

**Open problem: sources that are not websites.** Many kilns announce firings only in newsletters and on Instagram.
- *Newsletters:* planned path is Cloudflare Email Routing into a separate Worker (`kiln-worker/`) that stores the email text and runs the same extraction. Robby subscribes one dedicated address to each place's list.
- *Instagram:* Meta's official API only reads accounts that authorize the app, plus limited public data from Business/Creator accounts via the Business Discovery endpoint. It does not give general read access to other people's feeds, and scraping breaks Instagram's terms. Options to evaluate: (1) ask each place to authorize read access (best for willing partners), (2) Business Discovery for places with Business accounts, (3) Robby forwards or screenshots posts for extraction, (4) ask places to email the newsletter address. Verify current API terms before building.

## Systemic rules (added 2026-10-04)
- **Link health:** every crawl records whether each place's website loaded (`data/link-health.json`: ok / blocked / down). `kiln:build` drops the website link for any place marked "down" and adds a plain note, so the page never sends people to a dead site. "Blocked" (403/429) only stops our bot, so the link stays. The next crawl re-checks automatically.
- **Status:** a live Register/Sign-up button on a future-dated listing means "open", not "more info needed". Same rule is in the extraction prompt and the build.
- **Sort order (Firings view):** open now (by date), then ongoing access, sign-ups opening soon, waitlist, unclear, full, then places with no listings.
- **No dead ends in copy:** "Contact them to ask" only appears when a contact exists; otherwise the card asks visitors to tell Robby how to reach them.

## Evidence and deep links (added 2026-10-04)
Principle: every claim on the page must be something a potter can check in one click, and every outbound button must say what it really does.
- **Summaries are about firing only.** `place_summary` covers which kilns or firing types exist and how an outsider takes part. General classes, memberships and amenities are left out unless they are the way into a firing.
- **Evidence per firing type and per listing.** The AI must give a one-sentence reason plus a verbatim quote naming the firing type or kiln. The script checks the quote really appears on a crawled page and records which page holds it. Items it cannot back with a quote are not listed.
- **Deep links.** The "See the exact text" and "See it on their site" links use a browser text-fragment (`#:~:text=...`) built from the verified quote, so the page scrolls to and highlights the passage. Unsupported browsers just open the page.
- **Honest sign-up buttons.** The crawler passes the page's register/enroll links to the AI, which may only choose one from that list (`signup_url`, checked in code). If none exists the button reads "See it on their site" and goes to the highlighted evidence, never a generic department or apply page.
- **Fallbacks.** A listing with no evidence of its own uses the place's evidence for the same firing type.

## One action per listing (added 2026-10-04)
A listing card has one primary button and no redundant links. The button goes to the registration page if the crawler found one (`signup_url`), otherwise to the exact passage that shows the firing (`evidence_url`, text-fragment link). The evidence box quotes that passage on our side ("Their page says: ...") so the information is visible even if the browser can't scroll to it, and adds a link only if it differs from the button. Host website, newsletter and general links live on the place card, not on every listing. Never label a link "Sign-up page" unless it is one.

## Weekly automation, newsletter inbox, Instagram (added 2026-10-05)
- **Schedule:** `.github/workflows/kiln-weekly.yml` runs Mondays 15:00 UTC (and by hand). It crawls, rebuilds page data from approved items, writes `kiln-map/REVIEW.md` (`npm run kiln:review`), and opens a pull request on a `kiln-weekly/<date>` branch. It never pushes to main or deploys: the site changes only when Robby says "publish" and the PR is merged. Secrets: `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, `KILN_INBOX_TOKEN`, optional `IG_ACCESS_TOKEN` + `IG_USER_ID` (set with `kiln-map/setup-secrets.sh` and `kiln-map/INSTAGRAM-SETUP.md`). Repo setting "Allow GitHub Actions to create pull requests" must be on.
- **Newsletter inbox:** `kilns@flamingclay.com` → Email Routing rule → Worker `flamingclay-kiln-inbox` (`kiln-inbox/`), which stores each email in KV (180 days) and serves `GET /messages?since=` behind `INBOX_TOKEN`. The crawl matches emails to places by sender domain = the place's website domain, `contact.email`, or `newsletter_from` in sources.json. Unmatched senders are listed in the review. Evidence links use the newsletter's "view in browser" link when present; otherwise the quote is shown without a link.
- **Instagram:** Business Discovery via Robby's professional account reads the last 120 days of captions for places with `contact.instagram` (Business/Creator accounts only). Posts are treated as pages with the same evidence rules; the "Website" link goes to the post.
- **Link health (revised):** checks the host's main website, not the first crawl page. "Down" (link hidden) only when there's no answer or a 5xx twice. Any 4xx is "unclear" (could be bot filtering; some sites answer bots with 404): the link stays and the review lists it. The crawler never disguises itself to get past blocks.
- **Evidence must name the type** (`kiln-map/firing-words.mjs`): an evidence quote counts for a firing type only if it names that type or its kiln. Failing quotes are dropped (listing falls back to place evidence) and flagged in review. One evidence line per type. Place tags come from evidence; research guesses show only for places with no evidence at all. `includes` entries must appear in the crawled text.
- **Lessons from the first automated run (2026-10-05):** items whose evidence doesn't show a firing are dropped before review (for type "other" the quote must mention firing or a kiln). Ongoing access (classes, memberships, residencies) is matched without dates: a school's classes are one listing per firing type; memberships and residencies match on title. Newsletters sent through mail services match when the place's domain appears anywhere in the sender address, or via `newsletter_from`.

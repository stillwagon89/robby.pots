# Atmospheric Firings Lack a Living Map

No universal map shows every kind of ceramic kiln together. The tools that exist split cleanly in two. **Kiln Share** is a large, active, all-type rental directory: 1,288 kilns, about 15,000 monthly visitors (self-reported), and no filter by firing type. The **wood-fire maps** are small, one-person projects and mostly stale: Simon Levin's World Wood Kiln Map has 209 pins, and no entry mentions a year after 2019. Soda and salt kilns, which potters actively hunt for, have no directory anywhere. That is a real gap, but a narrow one. Demand for "find a kiln near me" is proven, mostly for electric firing by hobbyists. The atmospheric-firing audience is passionate, but probably numbers in the low thousands of active participants in the US, and it already finds firings by word of mouth. So a new tool provides value only if it does what the static maps cannot: filter by firing type, show upcoming firings and open crew or shelf slots, and keep itself fresh. A second "pins on a map" site would duplicate Kiln Share and Levin and fail the same way. The recommendation is to build a lean, atmospheric-first firing map and calendar as a community and brand project for Flaming Clay, not as a standalone business. Seed it by hand, partner with the existing curators rather than compete with them, and validate demand cheaply before writing much code.

## Electric is everywhere; wood, soda and salt are what potters go looking for

Kilns sort along three axes: heat source (electric, gas or propane, wood, oil, or combustibles in a pit or barrel), atmosphere (oxidation, reduction, or "atmospheric", where salt, soda or wood ash lands on the pots), and draft or structure (updraft, downdraft, cross-draft, and many wood-kiln forms). **Electric kilns are the most-used type overall**, easy to run and widely sold ([Ceramic Arts Network](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/freebies/guide/a-guide-to-ceramic-kilns)). They usually fire to cone 6 and cannot do reduction ([Ceramic Arts Daily forum](https://community.ceramicartsdaily.org/topic/20596-cone-9-10-electric/)). Gas is the most popular *fuel-burning* kiln and the cone 10 reduction workhorse of universities and community studios ([Ceramic Arts Network](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/category/firing-techniques/gas-kiln-firing/)). A typical university studio shows the pattern: the University of Kentucky runs five electric kilns plus an electric car kiln, one gas kiln and one 60 ft³ soda kiln ([UK Fine Arts](https://finearts.uky.edu/taxonomy/term/196)).

The atmospheric firings are a different world. Salt and soda kilns fire to around cone 10 and introduce sodium vapor that glazes the clay. Both need a **dedicated kiln**, because the vapor wears out walls and burners ([Digitalfire](https://digitalfire.com/glossary/salt+and+soda+firing)). Salt kilns have been shut down in many urban areas because of pollution rules, and modern soda firing grew out of Alfred University research in the early 1970s as a cleaner alternative ([Ceramics Monthly](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/ceramics-monthly/ceramics-monthly-article/techno-file--salt-vs.-soda-firing)). Wood kilns range from Japanese-style anagama, which fire for 48 hours to 12 or more days ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anagama_kiln)), to John Neely's train kiln, which uses less wood and labor than a tube kiln ([Utah State Magazine](https://utahstatemagazine.usu.edu/culture/campus-scene-the-train-kiln/)). They also include the semi-buried Southern groundhog kiln ([Folkstreams](https://folkstreams.net/contexts/the-kiln-and-its-firing)) and Fred Olsen-style fast-fire kilns that reach cone 10 in about 8–12 hours ([Ceramic Arts Daily forum](https://community.ceramicartsdaily.org/topic/40861-first-wood-fired-kiln-build-problem-stalling/)). Raku is a fast, low-temperature firing of 45–90 minutes per load, widely offered as an event ([Ceramic Arts Network](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/daily/article/Helpful-Tips-on-Purchasing-a-Raku-Kiln)). Pit and barrel firings run about 4–5 hours with combustibles ([PCC](https://www.pcc.edu/galleries/2010/08/01/multiple-artists-our-work-the-process/)). Waste-vegetable-oil kilns are a rare experimental niche ([Ceramic Arts Network](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/ceramic-recipes/recipe/Firing-with-Vegetable-Oil)).

| Category | Typical cone / temp | Firing length | Crew | Where it lives | How hard to access |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Electric (oxidation) | cone 06–6 | ~8–12 h | 1, unattended | Homes, community studios, schools | Ubiquitous |
| Gas reduction | cone 10 | ~12–17 h | 1–2 | Community studios, universities | Common in studios, rare at home |
| Soda | cone 10 | ~12–24 h | 2–4 | Universities, community studios | Moderately rare |
| Salt | cone 6–10 | ~12–24 h | 2–4 | Rural private, some schools | Rare, shrinking |
| Wood: anagama / noborigama / groundhog | cone 10–12 | 2–14 days | 10–25 in shifts | Rural private, art centers, universities | Rare, oversubscribed |
| Wood: train / cross-draft / bourry box / fast-fire | cone 10 | 8 h–3 days | 1–15 | Universities, private, backyards | Rare |
| Raku | ~1800–1900 °F | 45–90 min | 2+ | Studios, workshops, outdoors | Event-based, moderate |
| Pit / barrel / saggar | low fire | 4 h–overnight | 1+ | Backyards, festivals | DIY, easy |

*The table is a synthesis. Cone and duration ranges are typical practice, not figures from one source.*

The access ladder matters more for a map than the taxonomy does. Electric is so common that mapping it adds little beyond rentals. Wood, soda and salt are the "seek-out" categories, and demand already exceeds supply. Hambidge Center in Georgia fills 15 public anagama spots by lottery three months ahead and reserves 10 more for invited firers ([Hambidge](https://www.hambidge.org/anagama-kiln-firings)). Art Center of the Bluegrass warns that "all work may not get into the firing" ([Art Center of the Bluegrass](https://artcenterky.org/registration/anagama/)). Fees are modest, about **$125 for 2,000 cubic inches** at Hambidge, while crews owe one or more stoking shifts. The real constraint is **slots and timing, not money**. Studio Potter estimates "hundreds" of US wood kilns, with more now than 20 years ago ([Studio Potter](https://studiopotter.org/so-you-want-wood-fire)). No official count of wood, soda or salt kilns exists anywhere ([American Craft Council](https://craftcouncil.org/articles/kilns-that-build-community/)).

## Every existing map covers one slice, and the wood maps have gone quiet

The landscape is small enough to list completely. Here is how each resource scores on the three things a potter needs: kiln type, location, and when they can actually fire.

| Resource | Scope | Size | Freshness | Type filter | Firing dates / slots |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kiln Share | All types, rental | 1,288 kilns, 40+ countries, ~15k visits/mo | Active | No | No |
| Simon Levin World Wood Kiln Map | Wood only, 6 subtypes | 209 pins (~78% US) | No year after 2019 | By color only | No |
| Flash & Ash | Wood, with soda/salt tags | ~100+ listed, 84 mapped | Active (2025) | Tags | No |
| SideStoke | Wood (Australia/world) | Mirrors Levin | Frozen ~2010; creator died 2020 | No | Old events page |
| FindAPotteryClass.com | Studios/classes (US) | Undisclosed | Active | Raku, "outside firing" tags | No |
| Residency databases (AAC, TransArtists, CM list) | Residencies | 100+ (CM) to 700+ (Res Artis) | Annual / active | Gas, electric, raku only | Deadlines only |

**Kiln Share is the only resource with real traction.** It launched in April 2021, when its founder was a hobby potter without a kiln. It grew from 250 kilns and about 6,000 monthly visitors in January 2022 to **1,288 kilns and about 15,000 monthly visitors** today. It is free, takes no commission, and connects people through contact forms ([Ceramic Arts Network](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/daily/article/no-kiln--no-problem!-online-directory-connects-potters-with-local-kiln-owners); [Kiln Share](https://www.kilnshare.com/en/)). Its listing form asks for "Kiln type" ([Kiln Share sign-up](https://www.kilnshare.com/en/sign-up/)), yet the search map offers no type filter ([Kiln Share search](https://www.kilnshare.com/en/search/)). It is built around renting electric space, not finding a soda firing or an anagama crew. It also has press from Ceramics Monthly and Pottery Making Illustrated and a link from L&L Kilns ([L&L Kilns](https://hotkilns.com/support/pottery-kiln-videos-links/kilnshare)), so it would be a formidable competitor if it ever added type filtering.

**Simon Levin's World Wood Kiln Map** is the best-known wood resource. Its owners describe it as "a public resource of wood fired kilns around the world." It is a free Google My Map, color-coded by six wood types: anagama, fast fire, train, cross draft, groundhog and noborigama. Owners submit by emailing Levin directly ([simonlevin.com/map](https://www.simonlevin.com/map)). Parsing its KML export shows **209 placemarks**: about 163 in the contiguous US, 17 in Europe, 12 in Australia, and essentially nothing in Japan. **The latest year mentioned in any entry is 2019** ([KML export](https://www.google.com/maps/d/kml?mid=1I77OQo5qCeurI5h1XP_Kf8Y6azs&forcekml=1)). Bizen alone has about 300 operating kilns ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bizen_ware)), so Levin's map captures a small, aging fraction of the real world. SideStoke's world map is the same Levin map. SideStoke's own site has been frozen since about 2010, and its Australian creator Arthur Rosser died in 2020 ([SideStoke](https://www.sidestoke.com/)).

**Flash & Ash** is the liveliest wood-specific effort. It is a crowd-sourced Wix directory run by Detroit ceramic artist Any Guelmann as a passion project, with a free submission form and a beta map showing **84 kilns** ([Flash & Ash](https://www.flashandash.com/); [Flash & Ash map](https://www.flashandash.com/map)). It is the only wood directory that tags **soda and salt** ([Flash & Ash kilns](https://www.flashandash.com/kilns)). For interactive mapping, it still points users to Levin's map.

Outside these, firing discovery is scattered across single-town tours like the Seagrove Wood Fire Studio Tour ([Discover Seagrove](https://discoverseagrove.com/events/seagrove-wood-fire-nc-main-event)) and conferences like Woodfire NC, which next returns in 2028 ([Woodfire NC](https://www.woodfirenc.com)). It also lives on individual kiln owners' "upcoming firings" pages ([Gustin Ceramics](https://gustinceramics.com/?p=1699)). Residency directories treat firing type as prose. The Alliance of Artists Communities' equipment facet offers only raku, electric and gas kilns ([AAC](https://artistcommunities.org/directory/residencies)). Ceramics Monthly's 100+ residency listing mentions wood or anagama kilns only in free text ([Ceramics Monthly](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/ceramics-monthly/ceramics-monthly-article/2026-residencies-and-fellowships)).

**For soda and salt, nothing exists.** Searches turn up technique articles, not location lists. Forum advice to newcomers is simply to find "someone with a soda or salt kiln" ([Ceramic Arts Daily forum](https://community.ceramicartsdaily.org/topic/18935-lots-of-questions-about-soda-firing/)).

Popularity data for the wood maps is thin. Traffic for simonlevin.com and flashandash.com could not be obtained, and Instagram, Facebook and Reddit metrics were inaccessible to the research tools. On the evidence available, these are low-traffic reference pages, not destinations. Kiln Share's self-reported 15,000 monthly visits is the best measure of the whole "find a kiln" audience.

## Demand is proven for rentals and real but small for atmospheric firing

The "I need a kiln" problem is well documented. Kiln Share's founder reported that "demand for kiln hire significantly outweighs the number of kilns listed," especially in the Great Plains and major cities. In early 2022 the site was already generating "hundreds of firing requests" a month ([Ceramic Arts Network](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/daily/article/no-kiln--no-problem!-online-directory-connects-potters-with-local-kiln-owners)). Its roughly 5x growth in listings and 2.5x growth in traffic since then confirms that the need is growing. The broader hobby is large: **r/Pottery has about 268k members** and r/Ceramics about 138k ([GummySearch](https://gummysearch.com/r/Pottery/); [GummySearch](https://gummysearch.com/r/Ceramics)). NCECA 2025 drew a record 6,148 attendees ([NCECA](https://nceca.net/2025-press)). Studios in Boston and New York report long waitlists ([GBH](https://wgbh.org/culture/visual-arts/2024-12-06/ceramics-craze-boston-area-artists-share-why-people-are-flocking-to-pottery-studios)).

Atmospheric demand shows up differently: in waitlists and in complaints about being unable to find anything. A Danish 10-day communal soda and bourry-box firing ran a waiting list ([ceramic.dk](https://ceramic.dk/activities/wood-firing-soda-kiln-and-bourry-box-kiln/)). One forum potter had "not yet found anyone nearby with a smallish wood kiln" ([Ceramic Arts Daily forum](https://community.ceramicartsdaily.org/topic/33638-detailed-book-on-kiln-building/)). The American Craft Council notes that many wood-fire potters open firings to others for a fee or work exchange, and that word of mouth remains the main way to find them ([American Craft Council](https://craftcouncil.org/magazine/article/kilns-build-community)). That is the opening: the people are there and willing, but the coordination happens in DMs, email lists and closed Facebook groups that no one can search by location.

Be honest about size. The research found no search-volume data, no willingness-to-pay data, and no count of atmospheric firers. A rough, unsourced funnel runs from about 400k online potters, to tens of thousands needing outside firing, to perhaps a few thousand US potters who regularly join wood, soda or salt firings. A tool for that core is a beloved niche resource, not a venture-scale product. The comparables confirm the shape. UDisc began as a directory of 191 disc golf courses and grew to more than 16,000 because scoring gave players a reason to open it every round ([UDisc](https://app.udisc.com/blog/post/cheers-to-a-decade-of-udisc)). Mountain Project grew to 350,000+ crowdsourced routes before acquisition ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_Project)). Both won on repeat use, not lookup. Mountain Project's later fight with OpenBeta over a closed API shows how fast a crowdsourcing community turns on an owner who treats shared data as private ([Gripped](https://gripped.com/?p=39886)). On money, the market leader is free with no commission. The only sponsored precedent is Bullseye Glass's KilnSpace, a kiln-rental map run by a manufacturer ([KilnSpace](https://kilnspace.bullseyeglass.com/about/)). Charging listing fees is the hardest path. Supplier sponsorship and small fees on paid workshops or firing slots are the plausible ones.

## A differentiated version must be a calendar, not just a map

The value is not in pins. Levin's map shows what happens to a pin-only map run by one person: it decays quietly. Studio closures such as Clay By The Bay's overnight shutdown in July 2026 show how fast listings go stale ([Hoodline](https://hoodline.com/2026/07/clay-by-the-bay-kicked-to-the-curb-as-fisherman-s-wharf-pottery-hub-shuts-overnight-6730227/)). A version worth building has to do five things the incumbents do not.

The first is **type-first search across all firings**. Top-level filters would be electric, gas reduction, soda, salt, wood, raku, pit/barrel and other. Wood would split into Levin's six subtypes plus bourry box and catenary. Hybrids such as wood/soda would be first-class tags.

The second is **participation data on each kiln**. Each listing would show whether outsiders are accepted, the participation model (fee per share or volume, work-for-space, lottery, invitation, workshop or residency), cone, firing length, required stoking shifts and clay requirements. These are exactly the fields Hambidge and Art Center of the Bluegrass publish piecemeal on their own sites.

The third is **a firing calendar with open slots and alerts**: "soda firing within 200 miles, May, three shelves open." This is the repeat-use hook, the equivalent of UDisc's scorecard, and nothing in the market offers it.

The fourth is **built-in freshness and privacy**. Each listing would carry a visible "last confirmed" date and automated owner check-ins. Rural home kilns would get approximate locations and in-platform contact.

The fifth is **open data and partnership**. Seed the map from Levin's KML, Flash & Ash, university programs and residency listings, with permission and credit. Offer Kiln Share an integration rather than a rivalry. Make clear that owners own their listings.

The atmospheric-first scope also fits Robby's own position. A working potter with a "Flaming Clay" brand has more credibility curating firings than a generic directory does. The calendar piece doubles as a reason for the wood-fire community to know the brand. The cost is ongoing curation labor, which is the thing every prior one-person map eventually ran out of.

## Conclusion

The research turns the original question around. "Is there a universal kiln map?" has a clear answer: no. But universality is not what is missing. Electric rental is already served well enough by a free incumbent. What is missing is coordination for the scarce, social, scheduled firings: wood, soda and salt. Their whole discovery layer still runs on email, Instagram and who-you-know. The opportunity is therefore a firing calendar that happens to have a map, not a map that happens to list firings. Its success depends less on software than on whether Robby can keep a few hundred listings current and get kiln owners to post their dates.

The recommendation is a qualified **build, small and validated first**. Before writing code, spend a few weeks on cheap tests. Email Simon Levin and Any Guelmann about partnering or taking over upkeep; their datasets combined cover only a few hundred kilns. Hand-build a regional atmospheric-firing calendar for one hub, such as Northern California or the Southeast, as a page on the Flaming Clay site. Then measure whether kiln owners submit dates and potters sign up for alerts. Also check Google Trends and Instagram hashtag volumes by hand, since this research could not reach them. If owners engage, grow it into the type-filterable map and calendar described above. Fund it through supplier sponsorship, not listing fees, and treat it as community infrastructure and brand-building for Flaming Clay, not as a revenue line. If owners do not engage, that is the answer, and it will have cost almost nothing to learn.
